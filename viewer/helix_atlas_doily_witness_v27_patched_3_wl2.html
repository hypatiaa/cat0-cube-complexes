Top-down view of one Ω level. Disks are divisors at sum-of-contributions positions; outer ring is the factorial ring; center dot is the integer spine. Native Ω chords connect each prime's contributing divisors at this level as a polygon (vertices angle-ordered around the centroid); edges cross near multi-prime divisors. Envelope is the convex hull of all divisor positions across all Ω — atom-wide outline for visual classification. Circle layer: cylinder traces (opt-in) are the §9.7 contribution generators, a pencil of circles through the spine — the exponential lift's machinery. Ford circles render the rational skeleton instead (§9.1a register): each reduced phase a/q gets the classical Ford circle transplanted to the factorial ring (internal tangency at angle 2πa/q, radius R/(2q²)). On the line the radius law makes Farey neighbors exactly tangent (the Ford configuration is a degenerate integral Apollonian packing, curvatures 2q²). The flat ring transplant does not preserve tangency: distances stretch by 2π while the radius law keeps the line normalization, so det-1 companions sit visibly apart (≈×2π under-inflation plus curvature corrections) — det-1 contacts are therefore computed arithmetically on reduced fractions and marked with dark dots, never read from pixels. Two exact renders are available: Möbius disk (exact gasket — circles stay circles and kiss exactly, but the angle register is not preserved and the map carries an unforced gauge choice) and exponential lift (native FVLT — z → e^(2πiz), the §9.1a double coding: angle = 2πt exact, wrap intact, tangency exact by conformality; Ford circles render as horocycle ovals, congruent per denominator). Mediant depth recurses Farey mediants into the tangency gaps (Apollonian-style scaffolding, fainter per generation, excluded from the tangency count T). Both circle layers are atom-wide and do not depend on the Ω selection. Click a disk to select.
One rational skeleton, many bodies. Every reduced fraction a/q here is a single object under a single contact law — Farey adjacency, |as − cq| = 1. The registers render it differently: as an adjacency graph (combinatorial); as circles kissing exactly at Farey neighbors (Ford); as a gasket-style disk packing (Möbius view — tangency preserved, angle register given up); as tangent ovals wrapped by z → e^(2πiz) (exponential lift — tangency and phase preserved); and as beads on the lifted helix family, where by Lemma 9.3a contact can never appear as incidence and is drawn as edges instead. Instruments differ only in admission: the helix-family viewer admits fractions by Farey order (additive spine), this atom witness by cyclic orders (multiplicative content). Same rationals, same contact law, different geometries — no view is the object.
